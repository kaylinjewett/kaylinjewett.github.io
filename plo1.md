To demonstrate my foundational knowledge and comprehension of applied psychology subject matter, I chose to post my paper titled Movie Analysis: A Beautiful Mind. It was written in April of 2025 and shows the grasp I have on psychological terminology, theory, and concepts, specifically when it comes to abnormal psychology. This paper was particularly impactful to me because it put me out of my comfort zone. In my comfort zone I could rely on looking for right and wrong answers. Psychological disorders presented me with a grey area and pushed me to develop my confidence in decision making.

Abnormal Psychology examines behavior and determines what is abnormal. The American Psychiatric Association’s Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders 5th edition, Text Revision (DSM-5-TR; 2022), states that, “Although no definition can capture all aspects of the range of disorders contained in DSM-5″ (pg. 13) certain aspects are required. Some of those aspects include: dysfunction, distress, and deviance. 

My artifact explores a movie’s depiction of a man with schizophrenia. The movie shows the life of American mathematician, John Nash. The movie depicts his delusions and hallucinations, both positive symptoms of schizophrenia, as well as how they impact his family and friends. My paper describes John’s symptoms, diagnosis, etiology of the disorder, treatment for the disorder, and prediction of his future behavior. John likely has an underlying biological vulnerability that was activated by stress. He is treated by a psychiatrist and undergoes insulin shock therapy, medication, and hospitalization as he learns to cope with his symptoms. At the end of the movie he has learned to manage his symptoms and will likely continue to live in his new normal. The paper also has sections of empathetic attunement and my personal response to the experience. In the process of writing this paper, I had to refer to the DSM-5 to connect John’s symptoms with his eventual diagnosis (diagnostic reasoning). Additionally, I had to empathetically put myself in his shoes and consider how he might have felt as he experienced his symptoms (empathetic attunement). This paper taught me more than the basics of abnormal psychology; it gave me increased compassion and taught me how to relate symptoms to potential diagnosis. It allowed me to use what I learned in a real case and demonstrated comprehension of the topics introduced. 

Increased compassion and comprehension of the power of love and support in the healing process were the most impactful parts of writing this paper for me. I connected Alicia’s (John’s wife) willingness to live with John despite her fear and Martin’s (John’s friend) willingness to give John routine at Princeton with Galatians 6:2. This verse encourages us to bear one another’s burdens, something that Alicia and Martin demonstrated well. 

You might expect me to contrast this with personality psychology, but I found a stronger comparison with developmental psychology. I had recently completed developmental (lifespan) psychology when I completed my abnormal psychology class. Because these classes were completed so close together, I often found myself comparing the abnormal development to typical development. We could not understand psychological disorders if we did not first study typical development; developmental psychology gives us a baseline to compare the deviations to. For example, some symptoms of schizophrenia are delusions, hallucinations, and disorganized thinking (speech). Schizophrenia typically develops in young adults. According to developmental psychology, a typical young adult should be exploring their identity. They are actively maturing into who they will be and we see risk-taking behaviors begin to decrease as individuals prepare for long-term decisions. John’s experience demonstrates a young adult who veers away from the baseline as his delusions and disorganized thinking disrupted the process of typical development. 

Learning through PLO 1 has prepared me for my future career because it has taught me the basics of psychology and understanding the workings of the mind. abnormal psychology specifically has taught me about the nuances of psychological abnormality and how it can greatly impact someone’s life. As an aspiring Occupational Therapist (OT), knowledge of psychological disorders is crucial because of the impact they can have on occupations. OTs often work with patients who are experiencing symptoms of psychological disorders and it is crucial to know how to best adapt scenarios and interventions for your patients. One example this paper showed me was the impact of structured routines for patients, something I can carry over into my work as an OT. Additionally, my increased compassion for disorders that I did not understand before will serve me well as I seek to become an OT.
