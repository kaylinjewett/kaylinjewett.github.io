## Journey to Now

For as long as I can remember, I have wanted to help people. When I was a young teenager, I wanted to become a physical therapist. In high school, I thought about becoming a mental health counselor. Then, I gave up on those dreams for a while. After graduating high school and getting my associates degree, I never wanted to go back to school. 

However, a year later, I started working in a vocational transition program for young adults with special needs. During my first year working there, I realized that I really wanted to help people as a career. Through conversations with the Occupational Therapists, Speech-Language Pathologists, and Physical Therapists at my work, I realized I wanted to pursue a degree in Occupational Therapy (OT). 

This desire to become an Occupational Therapist led me to Biola just over two years ago. In researching OT, I discovered that Psychology is one of the five most common bachelors degrees for OTs and it would leave me the option to pursue psychology further if I ever wanted to. I have also found psychology and the way the mind works very interesting. It is hard to narrow down a favorite class or subsection of psychology because I have learned so much in each area. However, two of the most interesting topics to me were abnormal psychology and developmental psychology. These stood out to me because of the depth of the theories we covered and the ability to compare normal and abnormal development. Another thing about psychology that intrigued me was how it can be employed to help others. This was especially apparent as I studied the relationship between psychology and Christian thought, counseling techniques, theories of personality, and the psychology of marriage and family life. These classes and the work within them developed my critical thinking skills and my ability to apply something that interested me to real life scenarios. I knew that if I was interested in my major, I would enjoy studying it more. I had landed on a career path and a bachelors degree to get me there. All that was left was choosing where to start.

## Goals, Strengths, and Meaning

My main goal when I started this program was to learn as much as I could while getting through classes as quickly as possible. I started out seeing my BS as a means to an end. I wanted to get through the degree and move on to the next thing. Along the way, I have grown to enjoy the season and that striving to do well does not mean I have to stop living. I have learned that getting through is a fine thing, but it is important to stop and smell the roses along the way. Instead of changing my goal along the way, I added to it. I intentionally added fun things into my life, like spending time with friends, serving on church mission trips, and making time for my hobbies. I understand now how to learn without school consuming my life. 

I have grown in gratitude as I have learned to appreciate the struggle of working full time and taking classes full time. Five of my strengths that I have had to lean on in this journey are faith, kindness, gratitude, honesty, and humility. This journey has forced me to grow in these strengths. I also had to learn to ask for help: to depend on God and others, to be kind to myself and others despite being stressed, and to be sincere in what I put my mind to. 

I am so proud of how far I have come academically, spiritually, and personally throughout this journey at Biola. I look forward to sharing more about this growth and its impact on my life as you stick around!
